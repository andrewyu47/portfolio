# FOR Portfolio: how andrewyu.app works

This is the plain-language guide to the portfolio site. Read it when you come back in three months and wonder why anything is the way it is.

## The idea in one paragraph

The site is aimed at one reader: someone who owns demos, hands-on labs, and technical curriculum for developers. That work is mostly writing labs. So the site is built like a lab manual. The first thing on the home page is a one-question lab you can finish in a minute ("Who runs the tool?"). The centerpiece is a second lab that steps through a real, recorded conversation between code and the Claude API. The site is also something you can ask questions: it runs a small server that lets anyone's Claude read it as tools. The design looks like a two-color risograph print, the cheap and charming printing method that course handouts and zines use.

## How it was chosen

Four different designs were prototyped side by side, each from a different idea:

1. A manuscript being copyedited live.
2. A two-ink lab handout. **This one won.**
3. A lesson drawn on a whiteboard.
4. A museum exhibition.

Three reviewers judged them from screenshots only, never seeing the code or the pitch: an art director, a manager who would hire for this kind of work, and a teacher. The lab handout won two of three, because it was the only design where the visitor does a lesson before scrolling. The art director liked the idea and hated the execution (headline set like sticker text, no texture, too many boxes), so those were fixed in the real build. The best parts of the losers were kept: the copyedit moment opens Lab 02, and the teacher's notes shaped the agent figure.

## Then it was reviewed again, and it lost

The first full build went back to fresh reviewers, again from screenshots only. They scored it 5 to 7 out of 10, and the main complaint was fair: the site said "I teach live rooms" and never showed a room. Fifty rows of tidy type, no proof.

The fix was to stop describing and start showing:

- A frame from the session recording became a two-ink print of Andrew presenting, and 55 seconds of the same recording sits behind a play button, with captions.
- Every case study now opens with a figure beside its title: the clip, a chart of the eval results, a drawing of how a tool works.
- Every page opens with a flat band of teal, and each first screen gets exactly one field of pink on the thing that matters most.
- Numbers that two pages disagreed on are now printed once, or not at all.

## The look, and the rules behind it

- **Two inks, one paper.** Teal draws the apparatus: rules, labels, diagrams, buttons. Pink is the second ink: emphasis, data highlights, stamps, check marks. Where both print you get navy, and that is what all reading text is set in. Nothing else gets a color. The tokens are in `src/styles/tokens.css`.
- **Registration.** Real two-color printing never lines up perfectly. Headings are printed twice, teal over pink, with the pink a hair off. On page load the pink plate starts further off and slides into place.
- **Printing on arrival.** Figures print when you scroll to them: the teal plate wipes across first, then the pink lands. A figure asks for this with one attribute, `data-print`, and `src/layouts/Base.astro` does the rest. With reduced motion, or with scripts off, figures are simply there.
- **Texture.** A faint paper grain sits over the whole page, and solid pink areas have slightly uneven ink. Both textures are generated by `scripts/make-textures.mjs`, so they never change by accident.
- **Three typefaces.** Anybody (headlines, at two widths only), Literata (reading), Spline Sans Mono (labels and code).
- **Photographs are prints.** `scripts/make-portrait.mjs` and `scripts/make-photo.mjs` turn a photo into a halftone mask, so the ink color comes from the site's tokens. The room photo also gets "line work": the words on the slide are printed solid so you can still read them through the dots.
- **Dark mode is night printing** for type and rules: dark paper, light inks. Photographs do not follow. They stay light prints, like a page pasted into a dark notebook, because a face printed in light ink on dark paper looks like a photo negative. Headlines on dark paper are a plain light fill, because two light inks overlapping made a pink glow that looked like a different brand.

The written rules for anyone (or any AI agent) working on the site are in `AGENTS.md`. The short version: no boxes around things, no decoration that explains nothing, no em dashes, and no claim without a source.

## The parts

```
src/pages/index.astro            home: Lab 01, the four kinds of work, Lab 02, more work

src/components/explainer/        the agent-loop figure
src/components/figures/          figures that case studies show beside their titles
src/components/Photo.astro       a photograph as a two-ink print

src/data/                        profile, record, timeline, traces, labs, the two days

```

**Astro** builds the site. Almost everything is plain HTML and CSS that ships with no JavaScript. The interactive pieces (the agent figure, the rap player) are small React "islands" that load only when you scroll to them.

## The agent lesson, and why its data is real

`scripts/record-traces.mjs` runs an actual agent loop against the Claude API: it asks a question, lets the model request one of the site's tools, runs the tool, sends the result back, and repeats until the model answers. Every request and response is saved to `src/data/traces/`. The figure on the home page draws from those files and nothing else. If the API changes, record again and the lesson is current.

To record again (it makes about six API requests and costs a few cents):

```
node --env-file=<a file with ANTHROPIC_API_KEY> scripts/record-traces.mjs
```

`scripts/make-sample-traces.mjs` can write stand-in traces with no key. They are labeled as samples on the page, and the script refuses to overwrite a real recording.

## The site as an MCP server

`/mcp` is a small server that speaks the Model Context Protocol. It exposes three read-only tools: search the portfolio, get one case study, list the work. Anyone can add `https://andrewyu.app/mcp` to Claude and ask it about this site. The same three tools are what the recorded agent uses, so the lesson and the server can never drift apart.

The tools read one file, `src/data/generated/portfolio-index.json`, which `scripts/build-index.mjs` rebuilds from the page content before every build. The same script writes `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt`. One source, three outputs, so the pages, the server, and the text index always agree.

## Unlisted pages

Some pages are made for one reader and are reachable only by a link. They live under `/for/`, they are not in the navigation or the sitemap, and they are marked noindex.

Their content is not in this repository. The template, `src/pages/for/[slug].astro`, is generic. At build time `src/lib/unlisted.ts` looks for the content in two places: an environment variable on the host (`UNLISTED_PAGES_B64`, a base64 JSON array), or a file called `private/unlisted.json`, which git ignores. With neither, no unlisted page is built and nothing else changes. To update the hosted copy after editing the file, set the variable again:

```
base64 < private/unlisted.json | vercel env add UNLISTED_PAGES_B64 production --force
```

## The honesty machinery

Everything here is published under a real name, so a wrong number is worse than a missing one.

- Before any copy was written, a set of readers went through local files (resumes, course material, old case studies) and wrote a fact base: each claim with its source and a verdict. It lives outside the repository.
- Two fact auditors then read every built page against that base. They found numbers used for two different projects, plans written up as outcomes, and credentials nobody could source. Each disputed number is now printed once or left out.
- What happened at the two-day intensive is written from the index of the session recordings. Anything that comes only from a run sheet says so.
- The home page will not claim the labs repository exists until `src/data/labs.json` says `"status": "live"`.
- `scripts/check-copy.mjs` fails if it finds an em dash, a banned word, or placeholder text. `scripts/check-links.mjs` checks every link. `tests/site.spec.mjs` loads every page on a desktop and a phone and checks for errors, sideways scrolling, accessibility problems on both papers, and motion that ignores the reduced-motion setting.

## How to work on it

Build it outside any cloud-synced folder (builds hang inside them).

```
npm run dev          start the site at localhost:4321
npm run build        build it (runs the index builder first)
npm run check:copy   writing rules, after a build
npm run check:links  links, after a build (add --external to test outside links)
npm test             browser checks against the running dev server
npm run test:mcp     MCP server tests against the running dev server
node scripts/make-resume-pdf.mjs    reprint the one-page resume PDF (site must be running)
```

To add a case study, drop an `.mdx` file in `src/content/work/`. The frontmatter fields are described in `src/content.config.ts`. It will appear on the work index, the search tool, and `llms.txt` by itself. To give it a figure beside its title, add a component to `src/components/figures/` and name it in `heroFigure`.

Deploys happen on Vercel from the GitHub repository `andrewyu47/portfolio`: a push to `main` goes live. `vercel.json` tells Vercel the site is Astro (the project was Docusaurus before).

## Lessons learned

- **Judge designs from screenshots, with a reviewer who has not seen the code.** The builder cannot grade its own work. The harshest and most useful notes came from reviewers who only saw pictures.
- **A concept can win while its execution loses.** The lab handout idea was right and its first version looked like a costume. The fix was restraint: a 2 pixel offset instead of 14, one typeface at two widths instead of five, texture you only notice when it is gone.
- **Show the room.** A count of people taught is a claim. Fifty-five seconds of video is proof. The single biggest improvement to the site was one frame from a recording that already existed.
- **Two numbers that disagree are worse than no number.** A resume line and a case study each sounded fine alone. Side by side they gave the same 40% to two different projects. Nobody notices a missing metric, and everybody notices a recycled one.
- **Plans are not outcomes.** The course schedule describes what was planned. Only the recordings show what happened, so the pages are written from the recordings.
- **A public repository shows everything in it.** An unlisted page is not private if its source sits in a public repo. The content moved out of git, and the template stayed.
- **Background agents only know what you tell them.** Two agents stopped work because the last thing typed was about something else. The fix was to quote the real instructions in every brief and to keep anything that costs money or publishes in the main session.
- **Long jobs die when the network blinks.** Two overnight outages killed agents that had been running for hours. Shorter jobs with their own files lose less.
- **Shared data beats shared prose.** Numbers that appear in more than one place (103 people, the eval results, the lab list) live in one data file each, so they cannot disagree.
- **The old site is not gone.** It is tagged `docusaurus-final` in the repository.
