# LeCun, Lewis, and the Limits of Language

**An essay tracing two independent paths — Yann LeCun's compression argument and C.S. Lewis's "Transposition" — back to the same conclusion: text is a low-bandwidth medium that strips fidelity from reality.**

> **Role:** Sole author. Conceived, researched, drafted, edited, and published independently.  
> **Collaborators:** None on the writing itself. Intellectual debts to Yann LeCun, C.S. Lewis, Roland Barthes, and Thomas Kuhn, all cited in the post.  
> **Read it:** [LinkedIn — LeCun studied LLMs, I studied C.S. Lewis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/lecun-studied-llms-i-cs-lewis-both-learned-limits-language-andrew-yu-kogmc)

## Process

In 2022 I gave a Lightning Talk at Splunk titled "The Limitations of Language," not knowing LeCun had published "AI and the Limits of Language" months earlier. The post grew from noticing this coincidence and tracing both paths back to the same conclusion: text is a low-bandwidth medium that strips fidelity from reality.

I refined the structure over several weeks, weaving together a personal frustration, a paper I wrote at UC Berkeley, LeCun's compression argument, and the present hallucination problem. The goal was a piece that earned its conclusion rather than asserted it.

## Proud Of

This was driven entirely by my own passion. I saw a need, wrote about it, and discovered others felt the same way; I then used that existing interest as scaffolding to highlight my work.

The original contribution: I argued that LLMs hallucinate because they cannot experience the **Error Signal** that humans feel when a representation fails to capture reality. Hallucination as the *absence of dissatisfaction* — that framing is not in LeCun, not in Lewis, and not in any AI commentary I have read. That was my own connection, and I am proud I made it.

## Would Improve

I would like to deepen my understanding of knowledge graphs, a concept I only briefly touched on in the conclusion. I would also love to collaborate with one of the engineers who commented on the post to write a shorter companion piece aimed at people building RAG systems, to see if the "Transposition" language resonates with practitioners as well as it does with readers.
