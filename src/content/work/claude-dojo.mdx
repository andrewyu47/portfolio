## The room and the library

In July we had about 70 people at mixed skill levels, and a beginner room that promised no code and no jargon. If setup stalled, the rest of the weekend would turn into a demo.

The curriculum had its own problem. I co-authored it: 49 lessons on a ladder of eleven rungs, each lesson a short explainer to read or a prompt to copy and run, with 46 lesson videos. It is a deep, self-paced library, and two days in one room cannot cover it.

<Ladder />

To earn the top rung, you ship a working project and publish a 500 to 1,000 word write-up or a 2 to 3 minute demo video. Private work does not count, and slides do not count.

## Two days, one track

For the intensive on July 11 and 12, 2026, we pulled one guided track out of that library. Setup happened in the room on Day 1: after the opening talks, everyone installed Claude Code and had Claude interview them to write a context file.

<RunOfShow compact />

In my plenary I demonstrated the resume-tailoring Skill I use myself, then each person built a Skill for a task they repeat. Resume tailoring was one of several starters. Claude interviews you about your rules, writes the Skill file, and you run it on something real. Then you tell it what came out wrong and run it again. I was teaching that loop, and the resume was only the example.

Sub-agents and agent fleets were shown on Day 1 as a preview, and the hands-on build stayed with one agent. Anyone who wanted more had the rest of the ladder.

## The capstone

I opened it with one assignment: build a tool that proves you can do the job. For the capstone, people picked a real task, had Claude make mock data, and started building that evening in plan mode. My run sheet for the rest had me in the beginner room that night, and on Day 2 asking each person to make the tool shareable and then write one paragraph: the task, what they built, the result. That paragraph doubles as an interview answer.

## A rap for the fifteen terms

Fifteen new terms are hard to keep in your head after a full day of learning. So I made a rap with Claude that counts down the fifteen Claude Code terms a beginner meets first, in the order a learner meets them. I gave Claude the term list and the prompts, and Claude drafted and revised the lyrics. We played it for the room on Day 1, and I walked through the prompts that made it.
