## What it does

SAGE stands for Selection Agent with Guided Explanations. In a complex enterprise dashboard, a user runs into a metric, a chart, or an abbreviation they do not understand. Instead of typing a question into a chatbot, they draw a box around it. SAGE captures the DOM and HTML context of the box, retrieves the matching documentation, and answers in a popup next to the element.

I led the project and designed the architecture. It won the People's Choice Award and placed second overall at an AI hackathon.

## The problem

I call these moments paper cuts. A confused user opens a new tab to search the docs, asks a teammate, or stops using the feature. The product team hears about it later in a support ticket, often weeks after the frustration, and a ticket rarely says which button or label caused it.

## What the product team would get

The design also serves the product team. Each box would be logged as an analytics event in Pendo, and together the boxes would map where people stop to ask: the button, label, or chart they had to ask about. An ordinary heatmap records only clicks.

## From repeated questions to a ticket

When one element draws enough questions, such as "What does Health Score mean?", a threshold rule in the design opens a Jira ticket with the element ID, how often it was boxed, and the topics of the questions. The backlog would then fill with fixes that have a count behind them.

The Pendo and Jira steps were designed, not built.
