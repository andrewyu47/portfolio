I gave a lightning talk at Splunk in 2022 called "The Limitations of Language," without knowing that Jacob Browning and Yann LeCun had published "AI and the Limits of Language" a few months before. This essay traces both arguments to where they agree.

Browning and LeCun's argument is about compression. Text is a low-bandwidth medium, and it strips fidelity from the reality it describes. C.S. Lewis reaches the same conclusion by a different road in his essay "Transposition." I connect the two through Roland Barthes, Thomas Kuhn, and a paper I wrote at UC Berkeley.

The claim that is mine is about hallucination. When a person's words fail to capture what they mean, they feel it. Call it the error signal. A language model cannot feel that signal, and a hallucination is its absence. The essay closes with a brief look at knowledge graphs as one place to start.
